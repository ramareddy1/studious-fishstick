# Weekly AI Insight — 2026-06-19

## Title
**Scaling LLM Reasoning from Minimal Labels: A Semi-Supervised Framework with a Lightweight Verifier**

## Source
- **Paper:** [arXiv:2606.16811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16811)
- **Authors:** Keizo Kato, Chenhui Chu, Yugo Murawaki, Sado Kurohashi
- **Published:** June 15, 2026

## Why It Matters

Training LLMs to reason well typically requires large amounts of labeled data — expensive step-by-step annotations showing correct reasoning traces. This paper flips the script: it shows you can train a **lightweight verifier** on as few as a handful of labeled reasoning examples, then use that verifier to automatically judge and filter thousands of unlabeled reasoning traces generated by the LLM itself. The high-confidence traces become new training data, creating a self-reinforcing loop.

Key results:
- Achieves accuracy **comparable to using 10-15x more labeled data**.
- Uses **entropy-based confidence thresholding** to filter unreliable samples — only traces where the verifier is highly confident get used for fine-tuning.
- Tested on math reasoning (Orca-Math) and visual question answering (GQA with Visual Programming).

This matters because it dramatically lowers the barrier to improving LLM reasoning. A student or small team can label just 50-100 examples, train a tiny classifier, and bootstrap a much larger high-quality dataset — no massive annotation budget needed.

### Proof-of-Concept Script
A lightweight demonstration script is included in this folder: [`lightweight_verifier_poc.py`](./lightweight_verifier_poc.py)

The script demonstrates the core pipeline:
1. **Generate reasoning traces** — Simulate an LLM producing multiple reasoning chains for math problems.
2. **Train a lightweight verifier** — A small logistic regression classifier trained on minimal labeled examples (embeddings of reasoning traces).
3. **Entropy-based confidence filtering** — Only keep traces where the verifier's prediction entropy is below a threshold.
4. **Bootstrap training data** — The filtered high-confidence traces form a new, larger training set.
